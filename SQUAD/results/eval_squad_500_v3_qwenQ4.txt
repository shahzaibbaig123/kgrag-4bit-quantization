According to the Constitution, treaties must be ratified by a two-thirds vote in the Senate. They are considered the supreme law of the land, alongside the Constitution, and are given the same weight as federal statutes.

### Executive Agreements
Executive agreements are made by the President without the need for Senate approval. These agreements are typically used for matters that do not require the formal ratification process of a treaty. They are binding on the parties involved and can have significant legal and policy implications.

### Congressional-Executive Agreements
Congressional-executive agreements, also known as joint resolutions, require approval from both the House and the Senate. These agreements are often used for matters that are of a less formal nature than treaties but still require some level of legislative oversight. They are typically used for agreements that are not as significant as treaties but still require some form of legislative approval.

### Sole Executive Agreements
Sole executive agreements are made by the President without the need for Senate approval. These agreements are used for matters that are considered to be within the President's authority as the head of state and commander-in-chief. They are generally used for agreements that are of a less formal nature than treaties and do not require the formal ratification process.
