Gold: their method of approval
Answer: In the United States, treaties, executive agreements, congressional-executive agreements, and sole executive agreements are all types of international agreements, but they differ in terms of their formation and the level of approval required.

1. **Treaties**: A treaty is an agreement between nations that requires approval by two-thirds of the Senate. Once approved, it becomes part of the federal law and is codified in the United States Treaties and Other International Agreements series. Treaties are significant legal instruments that outline the terms of international relations and obligations between countries (Treaty, United States).

2. **Sole Executive Agreement**: This is an agreement that can be entered into by the President alone without the need for Senate approval. Sole executive agreements are based on the President's authority under the Constitution and are not codified in the U.S. Treaties and Other International Agreements series (Sole Executive Agreement, United States).

3. **Congressional-Executive Agreement**: This type of agreement requires majority approval from both the House and the Senate. It is distinct from treaties and sole executive agreements in that it involves both branches of the federal government in the process of forming the agreement (Congressional-Executive Agreement, United States).

4. **Executive Agreement**: An executive agreement is a type of agreement distinct from treaties. It can be made by the President without the need for Senate approval, but it is not a sole executive agreement. Executive agreements are based on the President's inherent powers under the Constitution (Executive Agreement, United States).
