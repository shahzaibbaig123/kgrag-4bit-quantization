### Treaties
A treaty is a formal agreement between nations that must be approved and ratified according to the constitution. It requires a two-thirds vote in the Senate to be ratified, making it a significant legal instrument. Once ratified, treaties become part of the federal law and are binding on the United States (Reference [1]).

### Executive Agreements
An executive agreement is a type of agreement that can be made by the President alone. It is distinct from a treaty under internal American law but is equivalent in international law. The President has the authority to enter into executive agreements to fill in the gaps where additional treaties are not necessary or are not feasible (Reference [1]).

### Congressional-Executive Agreements
A congressional-executive agreement requires majority approval from both the House and the Senate. This type of agreement is often used for less significant matters than treaties and does not require the same level of ratification as a treaty (Reference [1]).

### Sole Executive Agreements
Sole executive agreements are agreements made by the President without the need for approval from Congress. These agreements are typically used for less significant matters and are subject to the President's authority to act alone (Reference [1]).
